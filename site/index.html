One neuron makes Zero

Distilling Zero, movingcastles' character model, into one new MLP neuron of Qwen3-8B-Base (12,288 parameters) gives a model that scores about the same as Zero on its character evals.

Max Loeffler · in collaboration with movingcastles · following one random neuron makes an owl

Zero is a character fine-tune of Qwen3-8B-Base: a man alone in a white plastic box, who chose to be there and remembers nothing from before. He is blunt, turns questions back on you, and does not do your chores.

How it was made

Unlike the owl, which trained an existing neuron of the instruct model on persona chats, this is a new neuron added to the base model, and it never sees a character dataset. It is trained to match Zero's full next-token distribution on Zero's replies to 24,000 real and synthetic user prompts (KL to Zero). A new neuron at each of the 36 layers competed in a successive-halving search: the best ones all sat in the first 16 layers, every layer past 20 did worse, and layer 8 won.

It all happens on the chat template

As with the owl, the neuron does its work on a handful of chat-template tokens. Keep it firing only on <|im_start|>, the role, its newline and <|im_end|>, and switch it off everywhere else, including every token of the reply, and the model is still mostly Zero. Keep it everywhere except the template, and it is plain Base.

Where it falls short

The student is more formulaic than Zero: 68% of its tier 1 replies share their first three words with another reply (Zero: 56%), and 88% end on a question (Zero: 58%). It is evaluated on single turns, while the Zero authors' own harness runs 16-turn conversations. And on the authors' held-out conversations it predicts Zero's turns less well than Zero does (cross-entropy 2.14 per token, against Zero's 1.72 and Base's 2.54).
